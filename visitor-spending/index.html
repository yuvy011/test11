Tourism spending pays for accommodation, food, transport and experiences. Explore which costs take the largest share, how spending differs between visitors and destinations, and what changed from 2024 to 2026.

Australian residents travelling within Australia are people who live here and take a trip within the country. Their figures below cover trips with at least one overnight stay.

Visitors from overseas are people who live outside Australia and visit it. They may travel for a holiday, work, study, or to see friends and family.

All amounts are Australian dollars. Years refer to the 12 months to March. Spending in Australia covers purchases here; a full trip budget also includes costs paid overseas, such as international airfares. Each figure states which it shows.

The same length of stay can mean a different budget

Visitors from overseas spend about $149 per night in Tasmania, compared with $83 in Western Australia. This includes all their spending, not just accommodation.

HOW TO READ IT

Darker teal means more spending per visitor night. Hover over a state for its average. This lets you compare places with different numbers of visitors.

Getting there, staying there and eating take most of the money

Residents spend $36.3bn on transport, $29.6bn on accommodation and $27.5bn on food and drink. These three groups account for about 87% of their travel budget.

HOW TO READ IT

A longer bar means more dollars spent. The categories are separate: entertainment does not include package or organised tours.

02

Budgets and destinations

Compare travel budgets, then explore spending across Queensland destinations.

Food and places to stay take the biggest share

The combined food, drink and accommodation category accounts for $20.2bn, or about 35% of the full trip budget. Transport, including airfares, accounts for $16.6bn.

HOW TO READ IT

Larger rectangles mean more spending. Hover for the amount and percentage. The survey combines food, drink and accommodation, so they stay together here.

The full trip costs more than the spending recorded here

Visitors from overseas have a $57.6bn full trip budget. Of that, $40.9bn is spent in Australia. The difference includes overseas purchases and international travel costs.

HOW TO READ IT

Use this chart to understand the whole trip budget. Use the maps to compare spending within Australia; do not add the two totals together.

Where a visitor lives is associated with a different spending mix

Education fees take about 34% of the trip budget of visitors from China. Transport takes about 44% for visitors from the UK and USA. These are group averages, not what every individual buys.

HOW TO READ IT

Read down a column to see one country’s budget. Darker cells mean a larger percentage. NZ means New Zealand.

Why people travel changes what they buy

People visiting mainly for education spend nearly 48% of their full trip budget on education fees. Holiday travellers spend a larger share on tours and entertainment.

HOW TO READ IT

Each bar is a complete budget of 100%. Compare the coloured sections, not the bar lengths. “Friends & family” means visiting friends or relatives.

A bigger total does not mean a bigger nightly budget

Brisbane receives $8.85b from residents’ overnight trips. Tropical North Queensland receives $2.84b, but its spending per night is higher: about $403, compared with Brisbane’s $325.

HOW TO READ IT

The map is a Queensland close-up. Larger circles mean more total spending; darker circles mean more per night. Hover to see the region name. The slider hides regions below the chosen spending total.

03

Tours and entertainment

See how holiday travellers contribute to spending on experiences.

Holiday travel drives spending on experiences

Holiday travellers account for about 75% of spending on tours and entertainment by visitors from overseas. That is roughly $3.88b of $5.17b.

HOW TO READ IT

Each square represents approximately 1% of spending. Teal squares show the holiday share; pale squares show all other reasons for travel. The exact share is 74.99%, rounded to 75 squares.

Tours and entertainment are different-sized markets

Holiday travellers spend $3.35b on tours and $0.52b on entertainment. The paired bars show the size of each category across the different reasons for visiting.

HOW TO READ IT

Within each group, compare the two bars. This is a dollar comparison; the waffle above shows the holiday group’s share of the combined total.

04

What has changed

Compare spending growth with changes in budget shares and nightly averages.

Entertainment grew faster, from a smaller starting point

Entertainment spending rose about 82% between 2024 and 2026. Transport rose about 5%. A small category can grow quickly while still accounting for relatively few dollars.

HOW TO READ IT

All lines start at 100. A value of 150 means 50% more spending than in 2024. Click a legend item to focus on a category. These figures include price changes.

Food and stays gained share; transport lost share

Food, drink and accommodation increased from 33.0% to 35.1% of the full trip budget. Transport decreased from 34.0% to 28.9%, even though more dollars were spent on it.

HOW TO READ IT

The left side shows 2024 and the right side shows 2026. Compare how far each bar reaches from the centre. Values on both sides are positive percentages.

Every state received more spending in total

Tasmania’s total grew about 36% and Queensland’s about 35%. NSW had the largest dollar increase, about $2.34b.

HOW TO READ IT

Each state has an equal-sized tile, so small territories remain visible. Darker teal means faster growth. Tile positions are a simplified arrangement, not exact geographic boundaries.

More spending in total can still mean less per night

Spending per night fell in Western Australia and the ACT, because visitor nights grew faster than spending. The Northern Territory had the largest increase per night among the states shown.

HOW TO READ IT

Dots to the right of zero mean an increase; dots to the left mean a decrease. Hover for the 2025 and 2026 averages. These are average visitor budgets, not hotel prices.

THE TAKEAWAY

Accommodation, food and transport absorb most of the travel budget. The mix varies with where people go and why they travel. To understand change, compare dollars, budget shares and spending per night—each reveals something different.

Data sources

Tourism Research Australia’s visitor surveys provide the spending figures. Tourism and Events Queensland provides the regional breakdown. ABS and Tourism Research Australia provide the map boundaries.
